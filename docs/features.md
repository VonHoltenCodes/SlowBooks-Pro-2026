## Invoicing & Payments (Accounts Receivable)
- **Invoices** — Create, edit, duplicate, void, mark as sent, email as PDF. Auto-numbering, auto due-date from terms (the terms list's rule, else `Net 30` or a plain day count; terms that read as a negative count, such as `-0001`, need a due date given, or the invoice is refused with the date it would have had; anything else is due in 30 days and the invoice says so, see Payment terms), dynamic line items with running totals. Print/PDF generation via WeasyPrint. Inline customer creation from invoice form. **Draft means not yet marked sent, not unposted:** creating an invoice posts it to the books at once (A/R and income, and inventory-tracked items come out of stock); Mark Sent changes the status and posts nothing more. The form says so beside Create (v2.23.0, #293) **Quantities keep four decimal places** (v2.23.0): a line's quantity is stored to four places as unit prices are, shown as entered (2.125; 3.00 as before), refused finer, on invoices, estimates, credit memos, recurring invoices, bills, purchase orders and vendor credits — the imports stored a finer quantity and every read gave two places, so an edit took cents off the document
- **Line order** (#604) — On every line form (invoices, estimates, sales receipts, credit memos, recurring invoices, bills, purchase orders, vendor credits) each line has **Move line up** and **Move line down** buttons (dimmed at the ends), a handle to drag the line with the mouse, and **Insert line below**, which adds a blank line under that one. The totals follow, the order is saved with the document, and it prints, views and exports in that order. An edit that only reorders lines saves; over the API the order is the order of the `lines` array
- **Sales Receipts** — One-screen invoice + payment for point-of-sale style sales where the customer pays on the spot. Payment method, deposit-to account, and line items on a single form; posts both documents and their journal entries atomically. Imports from QuickBooks Desktop (IIF `CASH SALE`) and QuickBooks Online (SalesReceipt API)
- **Receipt scanning (Tier 2 OCR)** — A Scan Receipt button on the Sales Receipt and Bill forms uploads a receipt image or PDF and pre-fills the form from local OCR: date, merchant/vendor hint, and the grand total as a single line, with detected tax split out (tax-rate field on sales receipts; noted in Bill Notes). The operator always reviews before saving, and the source file attaches to the document. On Windows and macOS the OS engine reads the text and the OS renders PDF pages, so nothing is installed; on Linux, Tesseract + poppler-utils are user-installed system binaries called via subprocess — zero new Python dependencies, never bundled, graceful "install Tesseract to enable scanning" degrade. Deterministic parsing, no AI. Spec: [docs/design/receipt-intake-spec.md](design/receipt-intake-spec.md)
- **Estimates** — Full estimate workflow with convert-to-invoice: the invoice is dated the day it is made, due by the customer's terms, addressed to the customer (the estimate is addressed when saved). Inline customer creation from estimate form
- **Write Off** (v2.22.2, #255) — forgive an open balance in any company, not only a nonprofit: a credit memo to Bad Debt Expense, applied to the invoice at once, voidable to undo. From **A/R Aging**, **Write off balances under…** a figure you type lists the open invoices under it, each with a tick, and posts one credit memo per invoice together; a balance that changed since the list was made (a payment landed) is left alone and named, and a closed period refuses the batch
- **Payment terms** (#664) — a terms list under Settings > Payment Terms, the QuickBooks shape: a name and a rule, either **standard** (due a number of days after the date, 0 = on receipt) or **date-driven** (due on the next such day of the month on or after the date, or the one after it when the document is dated fewer than a set number of days before that day, as QuickBooks' "due the next month if issued within N days of the due date", N up to 28 so it fits inside February; past a short month's end, its last day), and either may offer an **early-payment discount** (a percent, to two decimal places, if paid within so many days, or by a day of the month; a day past the due day is in the month before). A new company starts with Due on Receipt, Net 15, 30, 45 and 60, each due as it always was; an upgrade adds a Net N entry for any other Net N already in use. **An invoice, bill, recurring or memorized schedule is tied to the term its terms named when the person picked them** (`payment_term_id`) and keeps that term's rule. A term added later never reaches one saved before it: saving it again with exactly the same terms (the forms send them back as stored) leaves it as it was, and any other text picked, even one differing only in case or spacing, ties it anew to the entry that text names (or to none). A duplicate and a schedule's next document keep their source's tie, and an import ties nothing. An upgrade ties a document only where the old parser read its exact text as that entry's days (`net   21`, `Net 030`), never where it fell back to 30 (`net<TAB>45`). Everything untied is read as the old parser read it, by every reader: the due date on an edit, A/P aging of a bill with no due date, the note, the PDF and the discount checks. A name the app already reads keeps its meaning (a term named "COD" or "Net 30" with another rule is refused, 409). The list's **Invoices & bills** column counts what was saved under a term, which locks its rule and name; **Customers, vendors & schedules** counts what carries its name and would take a change on its next document. Every invoice, bill, Bill Quick Entry, Quick Entry, customer, vendor, recurring invoice, memorized bill and the Default Terms setting offers the list, and every due date comes from one parser (`app/services/payment_terms.py`): the list first, then `Net N` and the due-on-receipt words. Terms it doesn't understand (free text from before the list or an import, such as "15th of following month") get the old 30 days on a new document, and the invoice or bill says what its due date is (`terms_note`: set 30 days from its date, or the date saved with it) instead of quietly; one saved before a term by that name was added says so. An invoice and a bill show the discount and its last day (`discount_percent`, `discount_date`, `discount_line`) on screen and on the PDF. A term on an invoice or bill keeps its rule and name (409); make it inactive and add another (DELETE is a 405 that says so). A recurring invoice on any receipt word (Due on Receipt, due upon receipt, COD, Net 0) is now due the day it is made (its generator read them all as 30 days). API: `GET/POST /api/payment-terms`, `GET/PUT /api/payment-terms/{id}` (writes are an administrator's; listing writes nothing), `GET /api/payment-terms/dates?date=&terms=&document=invoice|bill` (what the forms show as terms are picked; the year 1900 to 2199, terms up to 50 characters, and an unknown parameter is refused by name)
- **Payments** — Record payments with allocation across multiple invoices. Typing the amount fills the Apply column oldest invoice first (change any of it); **an early-payment discount** (#664): the Discount column suggests the invoice's terms' percent of its total (tax included, as QuickBooks takes it), at most what is still due, until its discount date, on the invoices the payment applies to, Apply fills the rest, and the discount posts to the account picked beside it (income, expense or cost of goods) as a credit memo applied to the invoice and tied to the payment (`allocations[].discount`, in whole cents, with an amount paid; `discount_account_id`), so voiding the payment voids it and the memo can't be voided alone. The server checks it against the invoice's terms: terms that offer one, a payment dated on or after the invoice's date and by the discount's last day (a prepayment dated before the invoice takes none, override or not), at most the percent of the total to the cent, and none taken before; anything else is refused in a sentence unless the request says `allow_discount_override`, an administrator's alone (another role is refused in a sentence); the form asks, then sends it, and the payment (`discount_override`, `discount_override_note`: who, on what, why), the memo and the audit log record it. One invoice twice on a payment with a discount is refused before any memo is numbered. A discount the customer took after a short payment is taken later with that payment (**Take discount** on the payment, `POST /api/payments/{id}/discount`), checked the same way on the payment's date, on the invoice's total, so a customer who paid in two instalments in the window still gets the whole percent. Money a payment's remainder paid later (Apply) takes no discount, there or here (`from_remainder` on the allocation). Concurrent requests are claimed first, so one stands and the rest are refused, and an `Idempotency-Key` replays the first answer. Sales tax owed stays the full tax (the discount posts no tax line); on the cash basis the discount settles its share of the invoice as a payment does, so both bases agree once it is settled, and an edit of the invoice recomputes the discount's cash lines with its payments', so the cash Sales Tax report agrees with Sales Tax Payable to the cent. It is not a sale reversed on the Sales Tax, sales-by-item or lifetime-sales figures, a foreign-currency invoice offers none, and an invoice with a discount taken keeps its terms and date (409) until its payment is voided; money left over is kept as a customer credit only when you tick the box that says so. Draft invoices can be paid too. Auto-updates invoice balances and status (draft/sent/partial/paid). Void payments with reversing journal entries — refused while the payment is in a deposit, and once that deposit is reconciled. **Retry-safe recording** (v2.23.0, #292): a client that may send a request twice — a form after a lost response, an agent, a script — puts an `Idempotency-Key` header on `POST /api/payments` or `/api/bill-payments`; the same key with the same body answers with the first response (`X-Replayed: true`) and books nothing more, the same key with another body is a 409 (the body is compared by its values, so 100 and 100.00 are the same amount and a re-serialised retry still replays), every keyed answer carries an `Idempotency-Key` header naming the key it was booked or replayed under, a first delivery still running is a 409 until it finishes, and keys are kept seven days. The app's own Receive Payment and Pay Bills forms send one per submit and ignore a second click while one is in flight
- **Customer credits** — Unapplied payment money and open credit memos are listed on Receive Payment, the customer page, the payment and the invoice, each with **Apply**; applying a foreign-currency remainder posts the realized exchange difference. A/R Aging, Income by Customer, the dashboard and every customer balance count them, so they tie to account 1100
- **Foreign-currency invoices** — The document carries its currency ("EUR 850.00") on screen, in lists, on the PDF and in email; the ledger books the home amount at the invoice's rate, and A/R reports count that booked amount. Receive Payment offers the customer's invoice currencies and takes the rate on the payment date
- **Recurring Invoices** — Schedule invoice generation (weekly/monthly/quarterly/yearly): an installment is made when you press "Generate Due Now" or **Enter All Due** (every missed installment in order, see Memorized transactions), or by the server's cron script; the app's own runs, when it opens and once a day, leave recurring invoices alone. A schedule keeps the day of its start date: begun on the 31st, it is dated the last day of a shorter month and the 31st again after it, a re-activated one resumes on that day, and one an earlier version had moved to the 28th returns to its day at its next installment. The form prices the item and shows a total; a schedule that adds up to $0.00 is refused, and the generator skips an old one
- **Memorized transactions** (#605) — A bill or a journal entry kept whole with a schedule, the way QuickBooks memorizes one: a memorized bill carries its vendor, terms, bill number and lines (account, memo, job, cost code, class, billable mark); a memorized journal entry its lines, reference, basis and adjusting mark. How often: daily, weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, monthly, every two months, quarterly, twice a year, yearly, or every N days, weeks or months; a next date, and an end date or a number of times. A monthly schedule begun on the 31st is entered on the last day of a shorter month and the 31st again after it; twice a month is the next date's day and fifteen days later. A recurring invoice holds to the day of its start date the same way. Two modes: **auto-enter** posts the document on its date: the app enters every due auto-enter memorized bill and journal entry when it opens and once a day while it runs, every missed date in order; when the company has a password, those runs wait until someone has signed in since the app started (a sign-in the app remembers counts, an API token does not), so nothing is entered behind the sign-in screen, and the audit log names the person signed in for what a run enters (an administrator or bookkeeper), or `scheduled run` when nobody signs in (a company with no password yet, the cron script) or only a read-only or an accountant's sign-in has (recurring invoices are not entered on their own: as before they wait for Generate, **Enter All Due** or the server's cron, so an upgrade posts nothing nobody asked for, and the page lists their mode as **Generated**, with a note naming those three), and **Enter All Due** on the page enters every due schedule of both kinds at once, every missed date in order however far behind (when another window generates one of those installments first, it keeps going, so the ones after it are still made); the page says what was entered while you were away ("Entered 3 scheduled documents while you were away", each a link; a run that entered nothing and had to skip something says "2 scheduled documents could not be entered", with the reasons), and the box under the list, **Enter due memorized bills and journal entries when the app opens and once a day** (`auto_enter_on_open`, on by default), turns the automatic runs off, for a server that runs `scripts/run_recurring.py` from cron instead. **Remind** lists it as due until you press **Enter now**, which posts it dated the scheduled date or today and moves the schedule on. An API caller sends the date it means as `occurrence` (with Enter now, `POST /api/memorized/{id}/enter`, and with a recurring invoice's `POST /api/recurring/{id}/generate`), as the page does: a date that is no longer the schedule's next one is a 409 and nothing is posted; left out, the schedule's next date at the time of the call is entered. In `GET /api/memorized` (and `/api/memorized/due`) a row is named by its `kind` and `id` together: an invoice row's id is a recurring invoice's (`/api/recurring/{id}`), a bill or journal row's a memorized schedule's (`/api/memorized/{id}`), and the two can be equal. Enter now names the occurrence the page showed (`occurrence`): two windows pressing it for the same occurrence post one document, and a page left open after that occurrence was entered elsewhere (another window, Enter All Due or the app's own run) is told so (409, naming the occurrence and the schedule's next date) and posts nothing, rather than entering the next one early; dated today, it still enters that occurrence. An occurrence later than the schedule's next date is refused too (409, saying it is not the next one and naming both dates), and nothing is posted. The schedule is moved on in one guarded statement before anything posts, on SQLite and PostgreSQL alike. A recurring invoice's installment is claimed the same way (`/api/recurring/{id}/generate` takes `occurrence` too), so two generations at once (two windows, Generate, Enter All Due or the cron script) invoice a period once: Enter now on an installment just generated is told so (409) and makes nothing, a run leaves it to the other, and a locked file is a 409 or a named skip saying the company file is busy saving another change, never an error. The bill number follows one rule: left blank, each bill is numbered the date and the vendor's initials, as Enter Bill numbers one (unique per day); a number on the template is used as it is on the first bill and -2, -3 after that, since a vendor's bill number cannot repeat; Memorize from a bill leaves the number blank on purpose. A frequency that carries its own count (every two weeks, twice a month, quarterly, twice a year) takes no interval: sent with none, or 1, it is that count, and with another it is refused, naming the word; daily, weekly, monthly and yearly take one (every N days, weeks, months or years). Pause and resume (resuming skips the dates missed, as a re-activated recurring invoice does); Enter now on an ended schedule says it has ended, paused or not, and so do Resume on one and ticking Active again on a recurring invoice past its end date (409, "This schedule has ended: its end date was ...; move or clear the end date first", naming the end date the schedule has and any end date sent with it that is still too early; a request that moves the end date past the next date, or clears it, goes through); a memorized schedule that ended by its number of times says to raise the number of times. The **Memorized Transactions** page lists every schedule in one place, recurring invoices included, with type, next date, how often, mode, last entered (a link to the document), status and amount, a Due filter, and Enter now, Pause, Edit and Delete on each; **Memorize** on a bill's page or a journal entry's page starts a memorized one from it. The posted bill is what Enter Bill posts and the entry what the journal form posts, so a closed period refuses them the same way
- **Documents that add up to $0.00** — An invoice, credit memo, duplicate or estimate conversion for $0.00 asks "Save it anyway?" before it saves (API: `allow_zero_total: true`, else 409 `zero_total`); a no-charge invoice starts Paid
- **Invoice numbering and footer** — Settings' invoice prefix, next invoice number (leading zeros kept as padding) and invoice footer are used
- **Saved invoice messages** (#392) — Settings → Invoice Messages keeps a short list of notes (how to pay, a thank-you), one the default; the invoice form's **Message** select copies the chosen text into Notes, editable — appended on a new line when Notes already hold hand-typed text, replacing them only when they are empty or still hold a saved message's text — and a new invoice starts with the default's text. The invoice keeps the text, not the choice, so editing a message later leaves old invoices alone
- **Walk-in sales** — A sales receipt needs no customer: counter sales go to a built-in Walk-in Customer (Anonymous Donor in a nonprofit)
- **Unit prices to four places** — $0.045 a box on invoices, estimates, sales receipts, credit memos, recurring schedules, bills, purchase orders, vendor credits and items; each line amount is rounded to the cent
- **Discount items** (#639): tick "Discount or concession: may carry a negative price" on a product or service item and a line on it takes a negative price on an invoice or sales receipt, the amount coming off the item's income account on either basis; any other item, or a line with no item, is refused as before (a refund belongs on a credit memo). The item a QuickBooks Online discount came in on is marked the same way, the items CSV export and import carry a Negative Price column, and an IIF `DISC` item imports marked. API: `allows_negative_price` on an item, and `is_discount` on an item response says whether a line on it may go negative
- **Credit limit** — Saving an invoice, editing one up, duplicating one (v2.31.1) or converting an estimate that takes a customer past their credit limit asks first, in the form and (v2.22.2, #256) on the API: 409 `credit_limit` with the limit and what they'd owe, saved only with `allow_over_credit_limit: true`. What they owe counts the credits and unapplied payments they hold, as A/R Aging does; a sales receipt, paid at once, is never asked
- **Batch Payments** — Apply payments to multiple invoices across multiple customers in a single transaction. Each line pays only its own customer's invoices, in the home currency; a batch with one wrong line is refused whole
- **Credit Memos** — Issue credits against customers, apply to that customer's invoices to reduce balance due. Picking an item fills its price; the form totals up; tax starts at the company's rate or the credited invoice's, and falls on the lines an invoice would tax. **Each line takes its own class and job** (v2.31.0, #640): the form draws the invoice form's Class cell on every line once the company has a class of its own (a nonprofit's funds always draw it); the reversal of that line's income posts with the line's class and job, the tax and receivable lines with the memo's, and a line with none takes the memo's, so P&L by Class and P&L by Job net a credit against the division or job that earned the sale, and a void nets each back to zero. Over the API a line's `class_id` and `job_id` are optional, and a line's job must be the customer's, as the memo's is. View, Save PDF and Print. Proper reversing journal entries. A memo that takes a stocked item back puts the quantity on the shelf and its cost back too, one entry per memo (`DR Inventory Asset / CR COGS`): at the original sale's cost, off the invoice's own sale postings and onto their accounts, when the memo names the invoice; at the item's average cost onto the item's accounts when it does not. Voiding the memo reverses both (v2.31.0, #641)
- **Vendor Credits** — Record a credit a supplier gave you for a return, a short shipment or an overcharge. Debits Accounts Payable and credits the expense (or Inventory, for stock going back), then applies to any of that vendor's open bills or sits on their account until there is one. Applying posts nothing — A/P moved when the credit was issued. Stock going back keeps Inventory at quantity × average cost, as a bill's void does (out at the cost the credit takes off Inventory, tax included, and back at that cost on a void), and a credit that returns more than is on hand says so in `warnings` ("Widget: 2 on hand; this credit takes it to -3."), shown as a toast, or is refused when *Refuse a sale of more than is on hand* is on
- **Invoices list sorts by any column** (v2.31.1, #650) — press a heading to sort by it, again to reverse; the headings are buttons the keyboard reaches, with `aria-sort`; the order chosen is kept when you come back to the list
- **Quick Entry Mode** — Batch invoice entry for paper invoice backlog. Save & Next (Ctrl+Enter) with running log. The customer picker has **+ New Customer** (v2.29.1, #484), as on the New Invoice form: a name, email and phone in place, Save adds the customer and picks it with the date, terms, PO and lines kept as typed, and the focus goes back to the picker (Enter in the quick add saves the customer, Escape closes it; Save & Next with it still open adds the customer first). A read-only or accountant sign-in is not offered it

## Accounts Payable
- **Vendors: search and paid-to-date** (v2.23.5) — the list filters as you type on name, company, phone and email, and `GET /api/vendors?search=` covers the same four (#569); a vendor's page shows paid this year and all time, with a payments CSV
- **Vendor payment history** (#569) — a vendor's page lists every bill payment, direct expense and bank-feed entry to the vendor in one **Payment history**, oldest first with a running total, over a date range that starts as year to date; voided rows are listed, marked and left out of the totals; **Export CSV** writes the same range through the CSV export service (Date, Type, Method, Check # / Ref, Amount, Running total, Paid from, Notes, Voided, Total paid). `GET /api/vendors/{id}/payment-history` returns the rows with `totals` by kind, `total` and `all_time`. The bill-payment subtotal is summed by the 1099 query; register entries that name the vendor count toward the 1099-NEC (#590) only when paid from a bank account, by their expense and cost-of-goods lines, so the bank-entry subtotal can be more than the 1099 counts of it. Direct expenses, which the 1099 does not count, sit beside them
- **Purchase Orders** — Non-posting documents to vendors with auto-numbering, live line amounts and totals, View, Save PDF and Print, and a To Bill step that asks for an account on each line that has none. A new PO starts at no tax
- **Bills** — Enter vendor bills (AP mirror of invoices). A posted bill can be edited the way an invoice can (v2.21.0): the header or the lines, re-posting its journal; not below what has been paid, not when voided, not into a closed period. Track payables with status progression (draft/unpaid/partial/paid/void). Each line posts to the account on the line, else the item's expense account, else the vendor's default — a line none of them names is refused (it used to fall back to 6000). Enter Bill has an Account column, fills a picked item's cost and account, takes the vendor's terms and due date, shows the vendor's summary beside the form (what you owe them, their last five bills and payments, their notes, and a link to their page), shows a running total and refuses $0.00. A line is an amount alone until a quantity or rate is typed (#391): a utility bill's line is what it says, and the API takes `amount` by itself, stored as quantity 1 at that rate. Save PDF and Print. Scan a vendor receipt to pre-fill the form (see Receipt scanning under Sales Receipts)
- **Bill Quick Entry** (v2.29.1, #487) — `#/bill-entry`: a stack of vendor bills keyed one after another (vendor with quick add, date, bill number, terms, lines of account, words and amount). Save & Next / Ctrl+Enter posts `/api/bills`, adds the bill to a batch log with a running count and total, and starts the next with the vendor, date and terms kept. The Account and Item cells have the account (#485) and item quick adds. Sidebar, top bar (Bill Entry) and the Bills page's Quick Entry; not offered to a read-only or accountant sign-in
- **Expenses** — A receipt that's already paid, by card, cash or check: DR the expense account, CR the bank, cash or credit-card account it was paid from, in one step with no bill to pay later. Scan Receipt fills it in. An expense takes a vendor or a payee, a reference and a class and job, and can span several accounts (v2.31.0, #599): its lines grid takes an account, a memo, a job, a cost code and type, a nonprofit function and class, and a billable mark per line, with a running total against the amount paid; the lines must add up to it, and one line looks and posts as an expense always did. **An expense is not edited, unlike a bill**: a mistake is voided (a reversing entry; both stay in the register) and entered again. Voiding is refused once the expense is reconciled or when its date is in a closed period
- **A new account from the purchase line** (v2.29.1, #485) — the Account cell on a bill, vendor credit or purchase-order-to-bill line, and the expense's account, has an **Add** button beside the picker: name, type (Expense or Cost of Goods Sold), an optional "Sub-account of" parent and an optional number. The new account is picked on that line and offered on the others; control accounts are never offered as a parent, and the keyboard goes back to the picker. The same panel as the register's category Add (#386). Not offered to a read-only or accountant sign-in
- **Tax on a purchase** — Sales tax a supplier charges is part of what the purchase cost: spread over the lines to the cent and posted with them (expense, cost of goods or inventory, in the item's unit cost). It never touches 2200 Sales Tax Payable. The Sales Tax report names purchase tax an older release posted there and gives the correcting entry
- **Vendor page** (v2.21.0) — click a vendor for its own page: contact, terms, 1099 status and whether a W-9 is on file (v2.22.2), the default account, notes edited in place, what's owed, its bills, its payment history (#569) and unapplied credits, each opening from the page
- **W-9 on file** (v2.22.2, #257) — a vendor's W-9 flag, next to the 1099 question on the vendor form. The 1099-NEC always printed "W-9 on file: No", because nothing could set the flag it prints. The 1099 Summary shows each vendor's W-9 and counts the reportable vendors without one (`vendors_missing_w9`)
- **Vendor list** — Balances worked out from open bills and unapplied credits; a vendor can be made inactive; cost-of-goods accounts can be a vendor's default ("Default Expense or COGS Account")
- **One bill per vendor invoice number** (v2.29.1, #501) — a vendor's bill number is unique, enforced by the database as well as the form, so two people saving the same number at once get "already exists for this vendor" for the second (409). Upgrading renames any pair already in the books ("111 (2)"); a purchase order turned into a bill steps past a number already used ("BILL-PO-0001-2")
- **Bill Payments** — Pay vendor bills with allocation. Journal: DR AP, CR Bank. **An early-payment discount** (#664): ticking a bill in Pay Bills suggests the vendor's terms' discount until its date and pays the rest; the discount posts to the account picked under the list as a vendor credit applied to the bill (DR A/P, CR the account) and tied to the payment, so voiding the payment voids it. It is checked against the bill's terms as Receive Payments checks an invoice's (`allow_discount_override` to take another, an administrator's, recorded), can be taken later with the payment (`POST /api/bill-payments/{id}/discount`), reads on the cash basis, is no purchase returned on Purchases by Item, and keeps the bill's terms and date. Pay Bills makes one payment per vendor and asks for one vendor at a time when a check number is entered; a payment pays only its own vendor's bills. A bill lists its payments, each with View, Print Check and Void (refused once the check is reconciled). A check written straight into the bank register, or entered as an Expense paid from a bank account, prints from its register row or the expense's view (discussion #354): the same 3-per-page stock, each split line on the stub. Paying from a bank account that would go below zero asks first. A payment that names no bank pays from Checking (1000); a chart without it is refused with a 409 naming the account, nothing written (v2.22.3, #284)
- **AP Aging Report** — Outstanding payables grouped by vendor with 30/60/90 day buckets past due, in home currency, with vendor credits and bill-payment money not yet applied netted, so the total equals account 2000. A bill with no due date ages from its date and terms

## Double-Entry Accounting
- **Chart of accounts: numbers optional, seeded accounts removable** (v2.23.5) — the account form says the number is optional; "Remove unused seeded accounts…" takes every seeded account with no postings, sub-accounts, register or control role off the chart in one step, naming the ones that stay
- **Use account numbers** (#567) — a box under Settings → Company, ticked by default (the setup wizard asks a new company). ## Payroll & HR
- **Core payroll** — pay runs with federal/state/FICA withholding, balanced journal entries, pay stubs, YTD totals, encrypted ACH direct deposit, gross-up calculator, supplemental wages, multi-state withholding
- **Void a pay run** (#432) — a processed run has a **Void** button that reverses its payroll journal entry (a dated reversing entry, both in the register) and the burden job cost it wrote, backs the amounts out of each employee's YTD and `BenefitYTD`, puts the time entries it paid back to approved-unpaid for the next run, and marks the stubs void (the stub PDF says VOID). Matched bank-feed lines go back to review, and a loan's balance goes back to the enrollment the run took it from. Refused inside a closed period, when a line of the run's posting is in a completed reconciliation, and when the posting was already reversed (the Journal page no longer voids a payroll entry: it says to void the run). A run whose direct-deposit file was already made, whose liabilities were paid since the pay date (a 941 deposit out of 2310/2330/2340, say), or whose quarter's 941 or year's W-2s, W-3 or 940 were generated after it posted asks first and lists why (`POST /api/payroll/{id}/void` answers 409 with the warnings; `?confirm=true` voids it anyway)
- **Opening YTD balances** (#433) — a company that switches mid-year enters each employee's year-to-date from the old payroll quarter by quarter (on the employee's page, or imported for everyone from the template), with the old system's taxable wages and each state's SUTA; each quarter's 941 carries only that quarter, the paycheck's Social Security, FUTA and per-state SUTA caps start from the taxable wages, and the 940, W-2/W-3, state SUI report and tax liability schedule count it; remittances and the journal do not. Once a run in the year is processed a change is a correction with a reason, and a draft figured from the old figures asks before it is processed (process it as figured, or discard it). The Social Security wage base is the year's own (2023–2026; a year with none here is measured against the nearest one, and says so)
- **Discard draft** — a draft pay run can be discarded from the Payroll page or its view (admin only): its stubs are deleted and what creating it changed is put back — the benefit year-to-date and loan balances its stubs moved, the time entries it took (free for the next run). A later draft figured while it counted is flagged. A processed run is never discarded
- **Taxable wages toward the caps** — each paycheck counts what it was taxed on, gross less Section 125 and HSA amounts (a 401(k) still counts), toward the Social Security base, the Additional Medicare threshold, FUTA and SUTA, as the opening balances do; the 941, 940, W-2 and state SUI report measure the same way. W-2 box 1 and 16 drop the pre-tax amounts that reduce income tax, boxes 3 and 5 those that reduce FICA
- **Direct deposit file** (discussion #211) — the bank's details are saved once under Settings → Direct Deposit (ACH): its routing number, name, the company account the pay comes from (encrypted at rest) and the two identifiers the bank assigns ("1" + EIN unless it says otherwise). A processed pay run then has a **Direct deposit file** button: the NACHA file to upload to the bank, named for the pay date, crediting each employee's active account (one still waiting on a prenote is left out) and debiting the company account for the total. The run's view says who the file pays and who is paid by check (`direct_deposit` on each stub). SlowBooks does not send the money; the business account needs ACH origination from the bank. `POST /api/payroll/{id}/nacha` still takes the details in the body, each field optional now, outranking the saved one
- **Payroll accounts** (#620): Settings → Payroll Accounts (administrator only) names the account each piece of a pay run posts to: net pay, gross wages, employer payroll taxes and each payable (federal and state withholding, Social Security, Medicare, FUTA, SUTA, other deductions). Each defaults to the seeded account it always posted to, so nothing changes until an account is chosen. Net pay takes a bank account, or a liability such as 2300 Payroll Liabilities to hold the wages until they are paid from the bank later (a check or register entry against 2300, a bill payment, or a journal entry); the Balance Sheet carries them until then, and the Process button's overdraft question is only asked of a bank. The save refuses an account of the wrong type, an inactive one, a credit card or a control liability in a sentence, a chosen account that stops working refuses the pay run rather than posting elsewhere, and a void reverses the accounts the run actually posted to. `GET /api/settings/payroll-accounts` lists the pieces, their chosen and default accounts and the accounts each may take
- **HR** — 8-task onboarding checklist with e-signature, time tracking (hours shown, approve or reject from draft, clock times work out the hours), PTO policies + requests with accrual draw-down (cancel a pending or approved request from the HR page: the hours it drew go back into the bank and its relief journal is reversed, the request stays on the list as cancelled; an employee withdraws their own pending request from the portal), pre/post-tax deductions, court-ordered garnishments. A pay run that would pay someone $0.00 is refused and names them and the time waiting for approval
- **Pay stubs** — a Stub PDF per employee on the pay-run view, naming the company; the view's Other column (state taxes other than income tax, garnishments) makes every row add up to Net. Processing a run that would overdraw the bank account asks first
- **Tax forms** — W-2, W-3, Form 940 (FUTA), Form 941 (FICA), 1099-NEC and 1096. JSON for downstream integrations + WeasyPrint PDFs with tamper-evident audit hashes in the footer (SHA-256 + audit ID matched against the `document_audits` table). The PDFs open by GET, as invoices do, so they appear in the desktop app's viewer. W-3, 940 and 941 count only employees paid wages; the 941 works lines 5a–5d from the rates and carries the rounding on line 7. A vendor marked "1099 Vendor: Yes" reaches the 1099-NEC and 1096. **Form history** (v2.22.2) on the Tax Forms page lists every W-2, W-3, 940 and 941 generated — what, when, by whom, its hash — and Check re-figures one from today's books and says in a sentence whether it still matches (a voided pay run in its period is said too); a footer hash can be looked up; a form generated before 2.29.0 is checked the way it was figured then, and said to come out differently today when it does. Admin only, read-only. **The W-2 has a line per state** in boxes 15–17 (the stubs by the state they were worked in, the opening balance's state rows), two to a form; a third state prints on an additional W-2 carrying only the state lines, as the IRS instructions say. The W-3's boxes 16 and 17 total every line
- **Self-service portal** — token-accessed at `/portal/{token}` — pay stubs, W-4 updates, direct-deposit setup, PTO requests (and withdrawing one still pending); branded with the employer's logo and company name

## Construction mode
- **One switch** — Settings → Company Type → Construction, the contractor counterpart to Nonprofit: it changes no data and is reversible. It adds the contractor accounts, the division/section cost codes and the default trade packages rather than re-seeding the chart
- **Contractor accounts** (#431) — Retainage Receivable (1150), Construction in Progress (1350), Retainage Payable (2050), Customer Deposits (2210), Use Tax Payable (2220), Equipment Cost (5400), Other Job Costs (5500); each created only if missing, its number yielding to a chart that already owns it, each pinned to an Other Current (job-cost accounts to Cost of Goods Sold) balance-sheet line. Idempotent: switching again or back changes nothing
- **Division/section cost codes** (#550) — divisions (`03` Concrete) and sections (`03 30 00` Cast-in-Place Concrete, optional `.SS` extension), the two-level numbering, each code carrying a `level` and a section auto-parented to its division. Typed codes normalize on save and import (`033000`, `03-3000`, `03.30.00` → `03 30 00`); a code that numbers its own way (`L-100`, `EQ`, a numeric `1000`/`2000` chart) is left exactly as typed and never rewritten on re-import. - **Trade packages** (#550) — the bid packages a GC awards (Concrete, Drywall and Ceilings, HVAC …), mapped many-to-many to the cost-code sections their costs post to; a package can span divisions and a division can be split across packages. Seeded with defaults on the switch; edited under Settings → Trade Packages (construction only). Nothing references a package yet
- **Cost type per line** (#550) — a cost type rides each budget, estimate, commitment and bill line (not fixed per code), chosen beside the cost code on bills, expenses, journal entries, purchase orders and estimates in construction mode (other company types keep their forms and post the code's type; the API takes a line type for any company); blank uses the code's default. Job-cost reports read the line's type first and roll a section's cost up under its division
- **The contract on the job** (#552) — a construction job carries a contract (lump sum, GMP or cost-plus) with a fee (percent of cost or a fixed amount, the fixed one winning), a cap, owner contingency and a retainage percent. A **Contract** tab (construction mode only) shows the terms, summary tiles (contract sum, changes, fee, cost to date, cap remaining, contingency remaining, billed to date and %) and the owner's **schedule of values** — the contract sum broken into billable lines (item no, description, package, section, kind, scheduled value), which must balance to the sum (the balance is highlighted until it does). **Build from accepted estimate** lays the schedule out from the estimate: one work line per trade package (base plus accepted alternates, markups already inside the values), an allowance line per allowance and a contingency line when set; the contract sum defaults to the bid total. Change orders (#554) move the contract and progress billing (#555) bills the schedule with retainage. Business and Nonprofit jobs are unchanged
- **Change orders** (#554) — how a locked budget, its contract and its commitments move, with an audit trail. A **potential change order** prices a change the owner hasn't approved (and **promotes** to an owner CO); an **owner change order** revises the contract; a **sub change order** appends scope to a commitment. Material and labor price separately, each with its own markup — the line's, else the CO's, else the job's **Markup on change orders** term — and allowance- and contingency-funded work carries **none** unless the CO's *Apply markup* flag is on. Numbers run per job (`<job number>-CO-001`, `-PCO-001`, `-SCO-001`); a PCO can be revised (the latest revision shows, the rest are history) and several PCOs roll up into one owner CO. An owner CO's **funding** is *contract* (adds to the sum, appends a read-only `change_order` SOV line and writes `source=change` budget rows), *contingency* (draws the contingency down, 409 `contingency_exceeded`) or *allowance* (spends an allowance line at cost, 409 `allowance_exceeded`). Everything lands on **approve** and reverses on **void** (409 `co_billed` when an added commitment line has been billed); a sub CO appends to an open commitment (same job; 409 when it is on another job or not open) and the buyout's *bought* grows. A **Change orders** tab (construction only) holds the log, the line form with live cost/sell per cost type, and the lifecycle buttons; the Contract tab's Changes tile goes live, its terms gain **Markup on change orders**, and the SOV shows `change_order` lines read-only with each allowance's used/remaining. Business and Nonprofit companies see none of it
- **Buyout and commitments** (#553) — a commitment is a purchase order: in construction a PO is a **purchase order** or a **subcontract** (Type select), carries a trade package, a retainage % and scope notes, and its lines are its schedule of values. **Partial billing** — *To Bill* offers an amount-this-period per line (prefilled with the remaining), each line tracks billed-to-date, and the commitment reads **partial** until every line is billed, then **closed**. A **retainage %** holds that percent of each bill's subtotal to **Retainage Payable** (2050) instead of A/P, so Pay Bills pays the net; a hand-entered bill can hold retainage too. *Committed* cost now means the **remaining** open commitment, with **committed total** and **billed to date** alongside in the job overview and cost tree. A **Buyout** tab (and `GET /api/jobs/{id}/buyout`) pairs each package's bid against what's been bought — variance, committed-remaining, billed-to-date and the commitments behind it, plus an Unassigned row and totals. Bills and Pay Bills carry each Sub's **compliance** flags (W-9, COI expiry) and a per-bill **lien-waiver** status, both warn-only. A subcontract to a vendor not marked a Sub is noted, not blocked. Sub change orders (#554), progress billing / retainage release (#555) and the buyout report / job cost by vendor (#556) build on it. Business and Nonprofit companies see none of it
- **Progress billing and retainage** (#555) — a construction job bills its owner from the schedule of values with a **pay application**: each line carries previous, this period, materials stored, completed-to-date, percent, balance to finish and retainage, and the application totals the owner's payment application (completed and stored to date, retainage to date and this period, total earned less retainage, less previous payment applications, current payment due, net due after a deposit). **Approve** creates the invoice for the job's customer — one line per SOV line billed this period (this period plus the change in stored materials) — and posts DR A/R net of retainage, DR **Retainage Receivable** (1150) held, CR 1150 released, CR income, CR tax; the invoice carries `retainage_held` / `retainage_released` and shows them. **Owner deposits** (`POST /api/jobs/{id}/deposits`) hold money on **Customer Deposits** (2210); a *deposit applied* draws one down as a payment to 2210 on approve. **Void** reverses the invoice and restores an applied deposit. On the sub side a **Sub pay app** bills a commitment a period at a time and **Release retainage** (`POST /api/purchase-orders/{id}/release-retainage`) writes a bill DR **Retainage Payable** (2050) / CR A/P, payable in Pay Bills. Lien waivers ride on both sides (an application warns about missing sub waivers for the period, and each payment received can carry conditional / unconditional, progress / final waivers that print as PDFs); retainage to date is net of releases, sales tax is on by default and can be turned off per application, and only the latest approved application can be voided; once billed, the schedule can't be rebuilt (409 `sov_has_billings`), a line can't shrink below what it billed (422), and a billed change order can't be voided (409 `co_billed`). A **Pay apps** tab (construction only) holds the grid, totals, deposits and Save / Submit / Approve / Void / PDF. Business and Nonprofit companies see none of it
- **The contract on the job** (#552) — a construction job carries a contract (lump sum, GMP or cost-plus) with a fee (percent of cost or a fixed amount, the fixed one winning), a cap, owner contingency and a retainage percent. A **Contract** tab (construction mode only) shows the terms, summary tiles (contract sum, changes, fee, cost to date, cap remaining, contingency remaining, billed to date and %) and the owner's **schedule of values** — the contract sum broken into billable lines (item no, description, package, section, kind, scheduled value), which must balance to the sum (the balance is highlighted until it does). The sum is **pre-tax** — tax is added on each invoice. **Build from accepted estimate** lays the schedule out from the estimate: one work line per trade package (its base lines plus accepted alternates, **at cost**), an allowance line per allowance, one line per non-zero markup (general conditions, insurance, fee, bond) and a contingency line when set; the contract sum defaults to the estimate's pre-tax bid plus any contingency, so a built schedule balances. A scheduled value may be negative (a deduct alternate). The Job Profitability report gains the contract type and a period fee (`fee_period`) for cost-plus/GMP. Change orders (#554) move the contract and progress billing (#555) bills the schedule with retainage. Business and Nonprofit jobs are unchanged
- **Construction estimating** (#551) — the estimate becomes a commercial bid: lines belong to trade packages, each is a base line, an allowance, an add/deduct alternate or a unit price, and carries a unit of measure. The **lines stay at cost** (a base/alternate line with a unit cost is priced at cost; one with no unit cost is priced direct and never marked up), and the four markups (general conditions → insurance → fee → bond) are their own figures **under the subtotal**, compounding in order. The subtotal is the included line amounts (base at cost + accepted alternates + allowances); the **bid total** is subtotal + markups, pre-tax; tax covers the lines and the markups together (nothing when the customer is exempt); the Total is subtotal + markups + tax. An alternate with a unit cost is shown at its marked-up price. **Accept** (`POST /api/estimates/{id}/accept`) seeds the job budget from the bought scope (an allowance budgets its amount as cost) and locks those rows — the lock is per row (a manual row on another code still saves), there's no unlock, and a second estimate is refused. Convert drops unit prices and un-accepted alternates, carries each line's cost type and unit cost onto the invoice, and adds one invoice line per non-zero markup so the invoice foots to the bid. The customer PDF groups by package with the section code, blocks the allowances / alternates (marked-up price) / unit prices, and foots Subtotal, a row per non-zero markup, Bid total, Tax and Total. Business and Nonprofit estimates are unchanged
- **Light vocabulary** — a contractor keeps Customer, Invoice, Estimate and Job; the one word that changes is Subcontractor → **Sub**, on screens and in prose. The API, database and exports never change name
- **Subcontractor vendor type** — a vendor can be marked a Sub, revealing a trade License #, and a certificate of insurance (Carrier, Policy #, Expires); the first time a vendor is marked a Sub, 1099-NEC turns on (only when its 1099 type is still blank, so a Sub already on 1099-MISC is left alone), and you can change it afterward. Un-marking a Sub clears the license and COI fields but leaves the 1099 answer as it is. A new vendor is not 1099 by default (W-9 on file stays a recorded fact, not a default)
- **Job reports** (#556) — seven construction reports lead the Report Center in a Construction group (the three that always existed — Job Profitability, Job Budget vs Actual, P&L by Job — then these five, which show in construction mode only), each a plain grid with a JSON route, a PDF and a CSV like P&L by Job, filterable by job, customer, status and include-closed. **WIP Schedule** (`/reports/wip-schedule`) lists each job with a contract — contract sum, approved changes, revised contract, estimated cost and gross profit, cost to date, percent complete, earned revenue, billed to date, over and under billing, retainage outstanding, cost to complete and projected gross profit — on the **cost-to-cost** method (percent complete = cost ÷ estimated cost, capped at 100%; earned = revised × percent; over billing a liability, under an asset), reading **as of the date** — cost and posted revenue to that day, billing and retainage from the applications approved by then, the revised contract with the change orders approved by then — with billing from approved pay applications when the job has one by the date else posted revenue (`billed_basis` says which); estimated cost is actual plus the **cost-to-complete forecast** (typed per job, section and cost type, default budget − actual floored at zero), so an overrun job with work left reads under 100%; a projected loss is booked in full (provision = loss × (1 − percent complete)); the **adjusting entry** is shown with amounts per job and net in the JSON, PDF, CSV and on screen — it posts nothing. **Cost-to-Complete Forecast** (`/reports/cost-to-complete-forecast`) is that table with PDF/CSV, edited in the Report Center or with `PUT /api/jobs/{id}/cost-to-complete-forecast`. **Commitment Status** (`/reports/commitment-status`) lists each PO and subcontract with committed, billed to date, retainage held and remaining, totals per job. **Cost to Complete** (`/reports/cost-to-complete`) is per job and cost code (division › section) — budget, committed, actual, cost to complete, projected final, variance and percent — a tree in JSON, flattened with the code path in PDF/CSV, totals footing the clamped rows. **Change Order Log** (`/reports/change-order-log`) lists every change order per job with the running approved total (as of the date) and the contract it builds, the pending total and counts by status. **Buyout Report** (`/reports/buyout`) foots the per-job buyout across jobs (bid vs bought, variance, committed-remaining, billed, Unassigned kept), as of the date. **Job Cost by Vendor** (`/reports/job-cost-by-vendor`) groups posted job cost by the transaction's vendor (a No vendor bucket for journals and job cost entries) with bills, paid to date, retainage held outstanding and open A/P; cost is period-filtered, the bill figures job-to-date and prorated by the job's share of each bill. Job Profitability and Job Budget vs Actual gained PDF and CSV routes. Business and Nonprofit companies see none of the seven
- **Not yet** — no first-run company-type prompt (Construction is chosen in Settings, as Nonprofit is), and Customer:Job still prints with a space. Pay Bills flags an expired/missing COI but does not block on it

## Analytics

Real-time business intelligence layer that sits on top of the accounting engine. Powered by `AnalyticsEngine` (`app/services/analytics.py`) with 8 aggregation methods and 7 REST endpoints at `/api/analytics/*`. **Fully integrated inline** into the main SPA as a hash-routed page — no separate shell, no full page reload. Click **Analytics** in the sidebar (under Accounting → Reports → Analytics → Tax Reports) to land on `#/analytics`.

**Metrics computed:**
- **Revenue by Customer** — Paid revenue per customer for the selected period, ranked high-to-low: invoices and sales receipts dated in the period and paid in full, before sales tax and in home currency — what the ledger booked to income for each (v2.22.3; it included the tax)
- **12-Month Revenue Trend** — Monthly income from the ledger (what was credited to income accounts, by posting date), bucketed by calendar month, with a month-by-month table under the chart; each point's figure sits beside it, never across its own line (v2.22.3; it was paid invoices by invoice date)
- **Expenses by Category** — Everything posted to expense and cost-of-goods accounts in the period, grouped by account and named as the Profit & Loss names it ("6100 - Auto & Truck Expense"): bills when dated (paid or not), expenses, register entries, card charges, payroll and journal entries, as the Profit & Loss counts them (v2.22.3, #281; it was paid bills only, so a bank-fed, journal-entry company saw nothing). Voided postings net to zero. A slice's figure is drawn only where it reads at AA; the table has every figure. An account that nets to a credit for the period (a refund larger than the spending) keeps its row and its place in the totals but has no slice. The screen and the PDF say the basis
- **A/R Aging** — Open invoice balances bucketed Current / 30 / 60 / 90+ days old, using `invoices.balance_due` so partial payments don't double-count. Table is sorted worst-offender first and includes a TOTAL row.
- **A/P Aging** — Open bill balances bucketed Current / 30 / 60 / 90+ days old (same treatment as A/R); as of a date, bills dated after it are left out (v2.22.3, #285). The 1099 Summary and the 1099 forms leave voided bill payments out (v2.22.3, #287)
- **DSO (Days Sales Outstanding)** — `(open A/R balance ÷ last-30-day paid revenue) × 30`
- **90-Day Cash Forecast** — 14 weekly cumulative buckets of expected A/R collections vs A/P payments due on-or-before each cutoff. Net column color-coded green/red.
- **Customer Profitability** — Lifetime paid revenue per customer (first pass; COGS attribution on the roadmap)

**Dashboard UI (`#/analytics`, inline SPA page):**
- **4 KPI cards** — Revenue (ledger), Expenses (ledger), DSO, Margin % — revenue, expenses, net income and the margin on one basis, the ledger's for the period (accrual, as the Profit & Loss), so the margin is net income over revenue (v2.22.3; it divided paid-invoice revenue into ledger expenses). The margin shows a dash when there is no revenue, and DSO a dash when no invoice or sales receipt dated in the last 30 days has been paid in full — the figure's denominator, which the card's hover spells out (there is no collection period to measure; it read 0.0). ### AI Insights
An optional LLM layer sits on top of the analytics snapshot and produces a compact **3 observations / 3 risks / 3 recommendations** executive brief. Nothing is sent until you click the **AI Insights** button — the feature is zero-cost by default.

**What stays local, and what does not** (#294). The company file and the REST API never leave the machine. The brief does: when you click AI Insights, the figures in the prompt go to the provider you configured, over public HTTPS only (private and LAN addresses are refused, see the SSRF boundary below), under that provider's data terms. The same is true of an agent CLI operating the local API from the [AI setup guide](https://www.slowbookspro.com/ai/): the agent's connection to SlowBooks is local, but a cloud-backed CLI's model is not, and whatever the agent reads from the books goes to its provider. A "no accounting data leaves this machine" setup needs a model you run locally, pointed at by the agent, and checked to make no outbound calls; running the CLI process locally is not enough by itself. Local credential storage says nothing about where inference happens.

**Eight providers supported out of the box**, Claude and Grok first:

| Provider | Wire format | Models | Free tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anthropic Claude** | `/v1/messages` | listed from `GET /v1/models` (active models) | Paid only |
| **xAI Grok** | OpenAI-compat | listed from `GET /v1/language-models` | $25 signup credit |
| **Groq (LPU Cloud)** | OpenAI-compat | listed from `GET /openai/v1/models` (chat models) | Generous free tier, no card |
| **Cloudflare Workers AI** | OpenAI-compat | listed from the account's model search (Text Generation) | 10k neurons/day, no card |
| **Cloudflare Worker Gateway** (self-hosted) | OpenAI-compat | listed from the gateway's `/models` if it has one, else typed | Same 10k neurons/day — **your keys stay in *your* Cloudflare account** |
| **OpenAI** | `/v1/chat/completions` | listed from `GET /v1/models` (chat models) | Paid only |
| **Google Gemini** | `generateContent` | listed from `GET /v1beta/models` (`generateContent` models) | Free Flash tier via AI Studio |
| **Custom (OpenAI-compatible)** | `/v1/chat/completions` | listed from the endpoint's `/models` if it has one, else typed | Any vendor or gateway that speaks the OpenAI wire format, on the public internet — HTTPS only, private/LAN addresses refused (v2.9, contributed by @jarvis4openclaw) |

**The model picker lists what your provider offers** (v2.22.2, #254). With a key typed or saved, **Settings → AI Insights** asks the provider's own models endpoint (`POST /api/analytics/ai-models` with what the page holds; `GET` for the saved settings) — Save keeps a first key with no model chosen and asks for one from the list — and lists the text models it returns, newest first where the provider says; you choose one, and **Refresh models** asks again. There is no built-in list and no built-in default, so a vendor renaming or retiring a model is never a code change here. **Custom…** still takes a typed model ID, which is also what a gateway without a `/models` endpoint falls back to. A saved model the provider no longer lists is kept and shown as Custom. Cloudflare gets an extra field for your account ID since its endpoint is account-scoped. The dedicated **Cloudflare Worker Gateway** provider adds a second field for your Worker URL — see the self-hosted gateway section below.

![AI Insights provider configuration](../screenshots/ai-insights-settings.png)

> **Which providers have run against a live key.** **Groq** was validated end-to-end against a live key at v2.0.0, both the AI Insights button and the predefined-analysis dropdown. **OpenAI's** request was corrected in v2.17.1 for its current reasoning models (#185, @Sciumo). **Claude** was run against a live key on the v2.22.1 release build (October 2026): Claude Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 (Anthropic's current models, the four the menu offers) and the legacy Haiku 4.5 each passed the Test button and AI Insights, and on Sonnet 5.5 a predefined analysis and a tool-calling question answered correctly. v2.22.1 brought its request up to Anthropic's current API: the request v2.22.0 sent is refused by Sonnet 5.5 ("`temperature` is deprecated for this model"). The others are built to each vendor's published API and unit-tested, without a live-key run by the maintainers. **Accepting working PRs that confirm or fix any provider's config** — open an issue or PR with provider name, working model ID, and any quirks discovered (e.g., headers, payload shape, error mapping).

**Settings encryption.** API keys are stored in the `settings` table under `ai_api_key`, encrypted with **Fernet** (AES-128-CBC + HMAC-SHA256) via `app/services/crypto.py`. Ciphertext rows carry the prefix `fernet:v1:` so legacy plaintext rows are detected and migrated gracefully. The master key is resolved in priority order:

1. `SETTINGS_ENCRYPTION_KEY` environment variable (ops-preferred; the desktop app keeps one in its per-user `.env`)
2. `.slowbooks-master.key` file next to the repo (an install that has one keeps using it)
3. Derived from `PAYROLL_ENCRYPTION_SECRET` when that is set to a real secret (v2.18+; how a Docker install keeps its key when the container is recreated). During a payroll-secret rotation the previous secret's key still decrypts, and `python -m app.services.encryption rewrap` re-encrypts these too.
4. Fresh generation on first run, written to (2) at 0600 (logged as a warning)

**Never commit `.slowbooks-master.key`** — it is in `.gitignore`. A secret no key on the install decrypts reads as not set (v2.18+): what uses it stops, and Settings names it to be entered again (`GET /api/settings/unreadable-secrets`).

`GET /api/analytics/ai-config` returns `{provider, model, cloudflare_account_id, worker_url, endpoint_url, has_api_key, api_key_provider, api_key_encrypted, providers}` — the raw key is **never** in the response body. `has_api_key` is true only when the saved key was entered for the current provider, and `api_key_provider` names the provider it was entered for. `PUT /api/analytics/ai-config` accepts a Pydantic `AIConfigUpdate` model — `{provider, model, cloudflare_account_id, worker_url, endpoint_url, api_key}` — so malformed payloads are rejected with a 422 before they reach the service layer. A missing `api_key` keeps the stored key, and an empty string removes it. **A key is sent only to the provider it was entered for** (v2.22.1): choose another provider and it has no key until one is entered for it, so one company's key never goes to another; switch back and the saved key is used again. A key saved before 2.22.1 belongs to the provider that was chosen when it was saved, read from the audit log; where the log has no such save, to the vendor its prefix names (sk-ant-, xai-, gsk_, AIza; a Custom endpoint on that vendor's own host keeps it); otherwise to none, and `api_key_unmatched` is true until a key is entered. AI Insights, the analyses and Test all answer 400 "No API key saved for <provider>" when the chosen provider has none.

**Endpoints:**
- `GET  /api/analytics/ai-config` — read display config (no secrets)
- `PUT  /api/analytics/ai-config` — update provider/model/key/account_id (key is encrypted on save)
- `GET  /api/analytics/ai-models` — the configured provider's models, from the provider (`{provider, provider_label, models: [{id, label}]}`); administrator only; the saved key is sent to its own provider only
- `POST /api/analytics/ai-models` — the same for what the Settings page holds: `{provider?, api_key?, cloudflare_account_id?, worker_url?, endpoint_url?}` override the saved values and nothing is stored; a typed key lists before it is saved
- `POST /api/analytics/ai-config/test` — one-word smoke test against the configured provider
- `POST /api/analytics/ai-insights?period=month|quarter|year[&force=true]` — run the full dashboard analysis; results are cached in-process for 10 minutes per `(provider, model, period)`, unless `force=true`

All calls go through a **hardened** `httpx.Client` with a 60-second timeout, `verify=True` (TLS cert validation), `follow_redirects=False` (no sneaky 302-to-metadata tricks), and a minimal `User-Agent`. Error messages redact the API key before raising, so logs and 502 responses never leak your secret.

**Security hardening (April 2026 external audit pass):**
- **SSRF guard #1** — Cloudflare account IDs must match `^[a-f0-9]{32}$` (regex enforced both at request-build time and in `PUT /ai-config`)
- **SSRF guard #2** — `validate_worker_url()` in `app/services/ai_service.py` rejects plain `http://`, embedded credentials, localhost, `127.0.0.1`, all RFC1918 private ranges, link-local (`169.254.x.x` including the AWS metadata endpoint), multicast, and reserved blocks. URLs are capped at 2048 chars
- **MITM protection** — `verify=True`, `follow_redirects=False`, HTTPS-only enforcement on the Worker URL
- **CSV formula injection** — `_csv_safe()` prefixes a user-controlled cell starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, `\t`, or `\r` with a leading apostrophe before writing to CSV, neutralizing Excel/Sheets formula execution. A cell that is only a signed amount (`-1234.56`, `-$1,234.56`) is left bare so it still sums; a value that looks numeric but is really an identifier keeps the apostrophe (a `+`-led phone number, a leading-zero reference like `-0001`, or any cell an exporter marks an identifier — the customer/vendor Phone columns, the IIF export's names and memos, the audit and closing-date-exceptions before/after columns)
- **Request schema validation** — `PUT /ai-config` uses a Pydantic `AIConfigUpdate` model instead of a raw `dict`, so malformed payloads are rejected with a 422 before they reach the service layer
- **Constant-time secret compare** — the shared-secret auth in `cloudflare/worker.js` uses a byte-wise constant-time compare instead of `===`, closing timing side-channels

### Self-hosted Cloudflare Worker Gateway (per-LAN-owner)

For installations where you don't want **any** AI credentials stored inside Slowbooks (even encrypted), the `cloudflare/` directory ships a minimal Worker that you deploy to **your own** Cloudflare account. Slowbooks only ever holds a shared secret scoped to your one Worker, so dumping the SQLite file doesn't expose enough to talk to Workers AI as you.

**What it buys you:**
- **Your keys never touch Slowbooks' DB.** Workers AI is accessed via the `env.AI.run()` binding — no Cloudflare API token is stored anywhere, in Slowbooks or in the Worker source
- **Per-installation isolation.** Every LAN owner installs their own Worker in their own Cloudflare account. One compromised install can't reach another; one abused install can't burn someone else's quota
- **Free tier friendly.** Cloudflare gives every account 10,000 neurons/day for free — plenty for hundreds of AI Insights runs and tool-calling Q&A sessions
- **Bearer-token auth.** Slowbooks sends `Authorization: Bearer <shared-secret>`; the Worker compares it against `env.AUTH_TOKEN` in constant time and rejects anything else with a 401
- **OpenAI wire format.** The Worker translates Workers AI's native response into OpenAI-shaped JSON (including `tool_calls`) so Slowbooks' existing OpenAI-compat code path works unchanged

**5-minute setup:** `wrangler login` → `openssl rand -hex 32` → `wrangler secret put AUTH_TOKEN` → `wrangler deploy` → paste the Worker URL + shared secret into Slowbooks **⚙ AI** → Provider: **Cloudflare Worker Gateway (self-hosted)**. Full step-by-step in **[cloudflare/README.md](../cloudflare/README.md)**.

**What it does *not* protect against:** a compromised Slowbooks install still has the shared secret, so it can still invoke *your* Worker — rotate the secret if you suspect compromise; abnormal Worker traffic shows up in the Cloudflare dashboard immediately.

## Online Payments
- **Stripe Checkout** — Accept online payments via Stripe's hosted checkout page. Customers click "Pay Online" in emailed invoices, pay on Stripe, and the payment auto-records with journal entries (DR Undeposited Funds, CR A/R)

- **Clearing account and processing fee** (#583) — Each provider has an optional clearing-account setting (off by default). Off, payments post DR Undeposited Funds / CR A/R as before. On, a captured payment posts DR the provider's clearing account (an asset created on demand — "Stripe Clearing" / "PayPal Clearing" / "Square Clearing", or one you pick) / CR A/R gross, and the processor's fee posts DR Merchant Account Fees / CR clearing in the same entry, so the clearing balance equals the net payout. The fee comes from the Stripe balance transaction, the PayPal capture's `seller_receivable_breakdown`, and the Square payment's `processing_fee`; when Square reports it late only the gross posts. A clearing payment stays out of Make Deposits. Set the toggle and the clearing/fee account pickers on each provider's card under Settings → Online Payments. When the net payout lands in the bank (#584), the deposit's **Payout…** button in the bank review queue — offered for a deposit whose payee names the provider — clears the clearing account for the gross and books any remaining fee, leaving the net in the bank; a payout without a feed is recorded with **Record payout** on the provider's settings card, which also shows the clearing balance awaiting payout. **Payout…** prefills the gross with the deposit's net (the clearing balance is shown for information), and both routes refuse a gross larger than the clearing balance. A deposit that already matches an entry in the books (a payout recorded by hand) offers **Match**, not a second posting. **Refunds and disputes** (voiding the payment) reverse against the provider's clearing account, never the bank; the fee the provider kept stays booked as an expense unless the void is marked fee refunded (`fee_refunded=true` on `POST /api/payments/{id}/void`); the Void confirm on an online payment with a booked fee offers a **The provider refunded its processing fee** checkbox (unticked by default) for that. Voiding a payment that looks already paid out goes through with a warning. Turning clearing off while the account still holds money warns, keeps **Record payout** and **Payout…** available until the balance is zero, and sends new payments to Undeposited Funds

## Inventory, Drill-Down & Duplicate Detection

![Inventory tracking on the item form](../screenshots/inventory-tracking.png)

- **Real inventory tracking** — Items can be marked `track_inventory=True` to hit a perpetual-inventory ledger. Every purchase (bill) and sale (invoice) writes a row to `inventory_movements` and updates `quantity_on_hand` + weighted-average `avg_cost`. Voiding or editing a bill takes each receipt back out at the cost it came in at (the line's rate with its share of the tax, what the bill put on Inventory), one reversal movement per receipt naming it, so the ledger's Inventory stays quantity × average cost (#646); a reversal that lands the count on zero, or is worth more than the stock holds, posts what the average cannot carry to the item's cost of goods account, so Inventory reads 0.00 with nothing on hand (#647). Stock that comes in while the count is below zero (sold ahead of its receipt) fills that hole first: the difference between what the units sold ahead were costed at and what they cost now goes to the item's cost of goods account under the bill, and what is left past the hole is stock at its own price, so after every receipt Inventory reads quantity × average cost (a sale's void, a credit memo's return and a count adjustment up fill the hole the same way). A bill's void or edit that takes stock already sold below zero says so in the response's `warnings` ("Widget: 2 on hand; this void takes it to -3."), shown as a toast on the Bills page, and is refused with nothing posted when *Refuse a sale of more than is on hand* is on. Each reversal movement names the receipt it reverses ("VOID Bill B-2 line 2") and `GET /api/items/{id}/movements` carries `reverses_movement_id`
- **Opening stock on a new item** — A stock item created with a starting quantity on hand posts it as one movement at the item's cost and one entry, `DR Inventory / CR 3900 Opening Balance Equity`, under source type `item_opening`. Dated the day the item is created or an `as_of` date you give (books set up as of a prior year-end); refused on a closed date. Only a stock item posts (not a service item with tracking forced on), a zero cost moves the stock with no entry, and a negative starting quantity is refused. The entry shows on the Journal page but is not voidable there — correct opening stock with an inventory adjustment. Opening Balance Equity is matched by account number 3900, so a renamed or QuickBooks "Opening Bal Equity" is reused rather than duplicated
- **Automatic COGS journal entries** — Selling an inventory item posts `DR COGS / CR Inventory Asset` at the current weighted-avg cost, to the item's own *Expense or COGS Account* when it is an active cost-of-goods account, else the chart's first COGS account (#622). Voids and quantity-down edits reverse each sale posting against the COGS and inventory accounts it was posted to, at its own cost (one reversal movement per posting, naming it), even if the item's accounts changed since. A credit memo's return posts the cost of what came back the other way, `DR Inventory Asset / CR COGS`, one entry per memo that the return movement links to (v2.31.0, #641): off the invoice's sale postings and onto their accounts at their own cost when the memo names the invoice (a return movement per posting, naming it), at the item's average cost onto the item's accounts when it does not; voiding the memo reverses the entry and the movement together. The P&L's cost of goods and Sales by Item's COGS column both net the return
- **Weighted-average cost** — Standard perpetual-inventory model: `new_avg = (old_qty × old_avg + received_qty × received_cost) / (old_qty + received_qty)`
- **Reorder points + low-stock report** — `GET /api/items/low-stock` returns items at or below their reorder point, worst-shortage first; the Items page counts them in a **To reorder** filter (v2.22.2, #261), listing each with what's on hand, the reorder point and how many short, and marks a short item's quantity in the full list. Off by default, and absent when nothing is short
- **Selling past what is on hand** (#642): a sale posts whatever the count is, as before, and now says so. Creating or editing an invoice, or entering a sales receipt, that takes a stock item below zero answers with a `warnings` list of plain sentences ("Widget: 2 on hand; this sale takes it to -4."), which the invoice and sales receipt forms show as a toast after the save; a line shows "N on hand" under its quantity once a stock item is picked. Settings has **Refuse a sale of more than is on hand** (`inventory_refuse_oversell`, off by default, under Invoice Defaults): on, such a sale is refused with a 422 naming the item and how many short, and nothing posts. The Items page shows a count below zero in the warning colour with a note saying what it means, the item's form, Adjust and History say it in words, and the To reorder list keeps including it
- **Item codes and quick-add from any line** (v2.29.0, #445) — an item can carry an optional code (`2x6-16`), unique when set without regard to capitals. Every line's item picker shows it before the name (`2x6-16 — 2x6x16 SPF`), so typing either finds the item; the Items page lists it and searches name or code, `GET /api/items?q=` matches either, and the items CSV export and import carry a Code column (QuickBooks' `!INVITEM` has no field for it, so the IIF export is unchanged). The item picker on an invoice, estimate, sales receipt, credit memo, recurring invoice, purchase order, vendor credit, bill or Quick Entry line has **+ Add item**: a small form in the line that saves the item and picks it on that line without leaving the document. On a sale it asks for a rate and an income account defaulting from the type; on a bill, purchase order or vendor credit for a cost and a cost of goods account (5000 to 5300 by type in the seeded chart; only COGS accounts are offered, since an item bought for a job or resale is never an operating expense), and whether a product or material is stock. It opens only when chosen (a name that matches nothing leaves Enter and Tab alone), Cancel and Save return the focus to the line's item picker, and a read-only or accountant sign-in is not offered it
- **Inventory valuation** — `GET /api/items/valuation` sums `qty × avg_cost` across all tracked items
- **Manual adjustments** — `POST /api/items/{id}/adjust` for count corrections, shrinkage, spoilage with an offsetting JE to #5900 (Inventory Adjustments) or COGS
- **Drill-down reporting** — `GET /api/reports/account-transactions?account_id=X` returns every journal entry hitting an account in the date range, with source-doc links (`/#/invoices/42`, `/#/bills/17`, etc.) so the SPA can jump from a P&L row to the underlying transaction
- **Fuzzy duplicate detection** — Customer/vendor creation warns with 409 on similar names (difflib similarity ≥ 0.85 after normalizing case, punctuation, and business suffixes like "Inc", "LLC", "Corp"). The form shows a confirm-and-create-anyway dialog with the matched names + similarity %; pass `?force=true` to override programmatically, or use `GET /api/customers/check-duplicate?name=...` for a pre-submit preview

Slowbooks can exchange data with QuickBooks 2003 Pro via **Intuit Interchange Format (IIF)** — a tab-delimited text format that QB2003 uses for file-based import/export.

![Interoperability page](../screenshots/iif-interop.png)

### Exporting from Slowbooks to QB2003

1. Navigate to **Interoperability** in the sidebar
2. Click **Export All Data** (or export individual sections)
3. For invoices/payments, optionally set a date range
4. Save the `.iif` file
5. In QuickBooks 2003: **File > Utilities > Import > IIF Files** and select the file

**What gets exported:**

| Section | IIF Header | Fields |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Chart of Accounts | `!ACCNT` | Name, type (BANK/AR/AP/INC/EXP/EQUITY/COGS/etc.), number, description |
| Customers | `!CUST` | Name, company, address, phone, email, terms, tax ID |

### Importing from QB2003 to Slowbooks

1. In QuickBooks 2003: **File > Utilities > Export > Lists to IIF Files**
2. In Slowbooks: navigate to **Interoperability**
3. Drag and drop the `.iif` file (or click to browse)
4. Click **Validate** — checks structure, account types, and balanced transactions
5. If the file has names typed in ALL CAPS, Validate shows a few of them as they would import and offers **Change ALL-CAPS names to normal capitalization** (`BOB JONES` → `Bob Jones`, `ACME TOOLING, INC.` → `ACME Tooling, Inc.`). It is off unless you tick it: customer, vendor and account names only, item names kept as typed, and names already in your books are left alone (v2.18.1, #195)
6. If validation passes, click **Import**

The importer handles:
- Automatic account type mapping (QB's 14 types → Slowbooks' 6 types)
- Parent:Child colon-separated account names
- Fields QuickBooks quotes because they hold a comma (`"Jones, Bob"`, `"99,250.02"`) — the quotes are not part of the name (v2.18.1, #195)
- Duplicate detection (skips records that already exist by name, in any case, or by document number)
- Per-row error collection (a bad row won't abort the entire import)
- Windows-1252 and UTF-8 encoded files

`tools/clean_iif.py` (source installs) writes a cleaned copy of an export with the same quote and capitalization rules, for anyone who wants the file fixed before importing it.

### IIF Format Reference

IIF is tab-delimited with `\r\n` line endings. Header rows start with `!`. Transaction blocks use `TRNS`/`SPL`/`ENDTRNS` grouping. Sign convention: TRNS amount is positive (debit), SPL amounts are negative (credits), and they must sum to zero.

```
!TRNS	TRNSTYPE	DATE	ACCNT	NAME	AMOUNT	DOCNUM
!SPL	TRNSTYPE	DATE	ACCNT	NAME	AMOUNT	DOCNUM
!ENDTRNS
TRNS	INVOICE	01/15/2026	Accounts Receivable	John E. Marks	444.96	2001
SPL	INVOICE	01/15/2026	Service Income	John E. Marks	-438.00	2001
SPL	INVOICE	01/15/2026	Sales Tax Payable	John E. Marks	-6.96	2001
ENDTRNS
```

## Sub-customers (Parent:Sub)
- **Sub-customers with parent billing** (v2.31.0, #638, from Discussion #633) - a customer can sit under another, one level deep: QuickBooks' "Parent:Sub". A property manager keeps each property as a customer and each tenant as a sub-customer under it, so a tenant is made inactive on move-out without touching the property, and everything rolls up to the property. The customer form has **Sub-customer of** (the active top-level customers; a sub-customer cannot have sub-customers of its own, and a customer that has them cannot become one, each refused with a sentence) and **Bill with parent**. Every customer picker lists sub-customers under their parents as "Parent: Sub" (`GET /api/customers?tree=1`; the API's `full_name`, `parent_customer_id`, `parent_name`, `bill_with_parent`, `sub_count` and `rollup_balance`), and a search for the property's name finds its tenants. A parent's page lists its sub-customers with each one's balance and the roll-up (`GET /api/customers/{id}/subs`: own, sub-customers, together), and a sub-customer's page names its parent with a link. Every customer answers three balances, each from its open documents: `balance` is what its own documents leave; `billed_balance` is what sits on its A/R, its own balance plus that of the sub-customers billed with it, the figure its A/R Aging row shows and what a property owes for its tenants (the same as `balance` for a customer with nothing billed with it); `rollup_balance`, on a parent, is its own balance plus every sub-customer's, billed with it or not. The Customer Center's list and page show `billed_balance` as the figure owed, the customer's own figure under it when the two differ, and the roll-up under a parent; the customer CSV export's Name column is the full name, "Parent: Sub" for a sub-customer, so two properties' Unit 1 rows are told apart. The credit limit checked when an invoice is saved (on the API, the form, an edit that raises what it leaves owing, a duplicate, and an estimate converted) is the payer's: an invoice to a sub-customer billed with its parent is checked against the parent's limit and `billed_balance`, and the 409 names the parent; a customer billed on its own is checked against its own. Making a sub-customer inactive leaves the parent as it is; making a parent inactive with active sub-customers asks about them (409 `active_subs`, naming each with its open balance) unless `?with_subs=true` takes them along, from the form and from Make Inactive alike. While a sub-customer billed with the parent, active or not, still has open invoices, the parent is not made inactive at all, with or without `with_subs` (409 `subs_billed_with_parent_owe`, saying what each owes and what to do: receive the payment or write the balance off, or take Bill with parent off the sub-customer), since the parent's A/R carries those invoices and no Receive Payment picker offers an inactive customer. A sub-customer is not made active again under an inactive parent, nor made or moved under one (409 `parent_inactive`: make the parent active first, or move the sub-customer); an edit that keeps an inactive sub-customer under its inactive parent is fine. **Bill with parent** puts a sub-customer's invoices on the parent's A/R: the invoice keeps the sub-customer as its customer and takes the parent's billing address, the printed invoice's Bill To names the parent and then "For: the sub-customer" (the whole Bill To block is frozen on the invoice when it is made, the name and country with the street: `bill_to_customer_id`, `bill_to_name` and `bill_to_country` on every invoice response, backfilled for older books from the customer each invoice was billed to at the upgrade, so a tenant moved to another property or billed on its own afterwards prints the invoice it was sent; an invoice moved to another customer by an edit, a duplicate, an estimate converted to an invoice, a generated recurring invoice and a pay application's invoice are billed as of that moment, the payer's whole Bill To, street, name and country, with the customer's ship-to, and an edit corrects the addresses with the bill and ship address fields of `PUT /api/invoices/{id}`, a part sent with a move winning over the one copied and a part sent as "" or null left blank, with a move or without, while a new invoice fills a part left out or sent empty from the customer and the invoice form sends no address part, so its save never blanks one; the Ship To's country is kept with the ship-to street the same way, the customer's own and never the payer's, as `ship_to_country` on every invoice response, backfilled for older books from each invoice's customer and given or corrected on `POST` and `PUT /api/invoices` like the ship street, so the printed Ship To, in the PDF and the print preview, keeps the country the invoice was sent with; and an invoice with a payment or credit memo applied to it, a write-off included, is not moved to another customer, the edit refused with 409 `money_applied`, whose sentence names each payment and credit, whose it is and for how much, and says to void it and enter it again where it belongs first (`applied` lists them), so the money stays on one customer's statement, while an unpaid invoice moves as before), the parent's statement carries the sub-customer's invoices, payments and credit memos (each line opens with the sub-customer's name), A/R Aging shows the sub-customer's balance under the parent's row (the row carries it once; `subs` on the row has each one's share), Email All Overdue and the collection letters go to the parent, Receive Payment for the parent offers the sub-customer's open invoices (`GET /api/invoices?customer_id=&billed_to=true`) and a payment from the parent applies to them, on the API too; a credit memo of the parent is routed the same way, issued against the sub-customer's invoice or applied to it, and the credit pickers offer the same invoices. A statement's payment and credit memo lines are what relieved the invoices on it, whoever paid, plus the customer's own payments and credits not applied yet: a sub-customer's own statement shows the parent's payment or credit that relieved its invoice ("from Maple St") and reads what the sub-customer owes, as `GET /api/customers/{id}` does; a customer with no sub-customers reads as before. A customer's own payment or credit memo applied to another customer's invoice (a former tenant's, or a tenant billed on its own, the shape the IIF import makes when it allocates a payment by invoice number) is listed as a pair, the payment and the invoice it paid named "for Unit 1: #1001" at what that money relieved, so the statement shows what the customer paid and its balance is unchanged. Bill with parent stays on, and the sub-customer stays under that parent, while any of the parent's money sits on an open invoice of the sub-customer (409 `parent_money_on_open_invoices`, naming the invoices and what is on them); once those invoices are settled the sub-customer may be billed on its own or moved, since tenants do move, and the statements keep the history: the former parent's statement keeps the invoices it paid and its payments as pairs, and the new parent's statement carries neither the old parent's payments nor the invoices they relieved (a sub-customer's invoice is carried on the parent's statement net of what a customer off the statement relieved on it, and not at all once that money covered it). The printed invoice billed to the parent prints the parent's country under the parent's street, and an invoice's `customer_name` is "Parent: Sub" on every response (list, get, edit, send, void, duplicate, write-off). Without the flag a sub-customer is billed and aged on its own, under its "Parent: Sub" name, and only rolls up on the parent's page. The IIF import, which cannot tell a QuickBooks Desktop job from a sub-customer (both are "Customer:Job" rows), asks: after Validate, a box says the file's Customer:Job names are sub-customers, and the import (`sub_customers=true`) then keeps "Parent:Sub" as a parent customer and a sub-customer with the row's own contact details, with the transactions that name it on the sub-customer; left unticked, each is a job under the customer, as before, and a transaction naming a sub-customer already on file lands on it either way. A bare name in a file is always a top-level customer, as QuickBooks spells it: an imported "Smith" is matched to, or made as, a top-level Smith and never posts to a tenant "Oak St:Smith", and "Smith:Unit 2" lands under the top-level Smith, never a third level under the tenant (a sub-customer is reached only as "Parent:Sub"). A name with more than one level ("A:B:C") is listed by Validate (`nested_customer_job_names`, with a warning) and imported one level deep, "A" with job or sub-customer "B:C", said once in the import's warnings. The QuickBooks Online export's Customer list carries no parent column, so its "Parent:Sub" names (the only way a QuickBooks name holds a colon) are imported as a parent and a sub-customer, and the A/R Aging Detail's open invoices for "Parent:Sub" land on the sub-customer. The IIF export writes a sub-customer as "Parent:Sub" on every row: the customer list (where it follows its parent, the top-level rows in their usual name order) and the NAME of every invoice, payment, estimate and sales receipt TRNS and SPL row. A new sub-customer's name is checked for a likely duplicate against the top-level names, and among its siblings under the same parent only the same name, in any case, is refused (`GET /api/customers/check-duplicate?name=&parent_customer_id=`), so every property can have a Unit 1 and Unit 2 beside Unit 1 is the next unit, not a likely typo of it; a top-level name is checked the likely-match way against the top-level names, as before. In the Customer Center every sub-customer listed right under its parent shows its own name, however many there are; a sub-customer whose parent is not in the list shows its full name.
